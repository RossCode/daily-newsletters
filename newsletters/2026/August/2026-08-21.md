## Top Stories

### [Slack Code turns coding into a group activity](https://www.theverge.com/tech/982628/slack-code-vibe-coding-channels-launch)
*(4 newsletters)*

Salesforce launched Slack Code, which puts coding agents inside shared, project-specific Slack channels where engineers and non-engineers can watch, steer, and approve a build in one place. Agents including Claude, ChatGPT, Devin, Vercel, and GitHub can be added to a channel, deploys stay gated behind human approval, and finished channels archive themselves into a searchable record. It is live on all Slack plans, and the framing is telling: rather than compete on building the best agent, Slack is trying to own the venue where human and AI workers meet.

### [Ramp launches Router, its own AI model router](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/ramp-launches-its-own-ai-model-router-called-router/)
*(3 newsletters)*

Ramp shipped Router, a single endpoint that sends each request to the cheapest model meeting a stated quality bar, reacting to live latency and failure rates and falling back automatically when a provider degrades. It claims roughly 40 percent average cost reduction, is compatible with both the OpenAI and Anthropic APIs so switching takes one line, and is free through 2026 with starter credits. A fintech shipping inference plumbing is a signal in itself: model cost is now a line item companies build tooling around.

### [Stripe tells investors the singularity already started](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/19/stripe-payments-openrouter-singularity)
*(2 newsletters)*

In its August investor letter, Stripe argued that January 1 marked an inflection point across several long-running trends and set out a goal of accelerating AI diffusion through the economy, making it one of the first non-AI-lab companies to make that claim publicly. The letter lands alongside its acquisition of OpenRouter, which routes over 10 trillion tokens a day. Analysis circulating this week reads the deal less as distribution and more as a data play: cross-network behavioral data no single provider can match, with Stripe positioned as the neutral party holding it.

### [OpenAI previews Private Safety Processing for zero-data-retention customers](https://openai.com/index/offering-zero-data-retention-for-frontier-models/)
*(2 newsletters)*

OpenAI is previewing a system that detects harmful patterns across related interactions while giving its own personnel no access to the underlying prompts or responses, aimed at eligible Zero Data Retention API customers. Content can stay on customer-controlled infrastructure, automated systems receive only limited safety signals, and customer-managed encryption keys are in development. It is a direct answer to the tension between multi-turn abuse detection and enterprise or regulatory demands that sensitive prompts never be retained.

### [Cursor cloud agents now run until the job is done](https://cursor.com/changelog/08-19-26)
*(2 newsletters)*

Cursor's cloud agents gained subscriptions to event sources, long-lived goals, custom modes, and isolated subagents on their own virtual machines. An agent can now watch a pull request or a Slack thread, wake when something happens, fix a CI failure, and keep going toward an objective without a human closing every loop. Users can also steer a running agent by message rather than interrupting it.

### [Asana used Codex to finish a five-year migration in two weeks](https://openai.com/index/asana/)
*(2 newsletters)*

Asana says it removed an outdated testing framework using OpenAI's Codex in about two weeks for roughly $12,000, against an internal estimate of five years and $6 million. The number is a vendor-published case study and should be read as such, but the shape of the claim matters: the work was large, mechanical, and well specified, which is exactly the profile where agent harnesses are currently delivering.

### [Meta ships a macOS desktop app for Meta AI](https://www.testingcatalog.com/meta-launches-ai-desktop-app-for-macos-with-screen-sharing/)
*(2 newsletters)*

Meta released a Mac desktop app for Meta AI with screen sharing, able to pull from public social posts and the web and to help business owners interpret their Meta ad performance. It follows July's Muse release, the first model family out of Alexandr Wang's Meta Superintelligence Labs. Separately, Meta's Muse Video model entered closed beta with native audio and 10-second clips showing strong temporal consistency.

### [Replit Free Mode gives Core subscribers 30x more capacity](https://replit.com/blog/replit-introduces-free-mode)
*(2 newsletters)*

Replit launched Free Mode, powered by OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Luna, letting Core subscribers build and chat roughly 30 times more without burning credits on everyday tasks. The pitch is affordability for students, solo developers, and small teams, with a reworked interface that moves from chat into full builds. Another instance of the week's real theme: cheap models absorbing routine work so frontier spend goes only where it earns its price.

## Also Worth Knowing

- **Anthropic is preparing a mega-IPO.** A filing could come as soon as the end of this month, sized to match or beat SpaceX's record debut, with a credit facility above its roughly $10 billion target and super-voting shares under consideration for the co-founders.
- **[OpenAI paused reinforcement learning on its newest model](https://openai.com/index/pacing-model-development-cyber-capabilities/).** Sam Altman said capabilities were outstripping the pace of safety and alignment work, the lab's first deliberate training slowdown, though it still plans to ship soon.
- **[Charter closed its $34.5 billion Cox acquisition](https://qz.com/charter-cox-communications-merger-closed-082026).** The combined company is now the largest US internet and video provider at 37 million subscribers, and it closed the all-stock Liberty Broadband deal at the same time.
- **[Micron is putting $10 billion into an AI memory research lab in Boise](https://qz.com/micron-research-labs-ai-memory-boise-082026).** Micron Research Labs will run over a decade and pull in customers, universities, and government agencies around memory, compute architecture, and packaging.
- **[Canva cut AI serving costs about 90 percent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9uq9zxtwBrk).** After nine profitable years it trimmed 2026 growth guidance from 30 to 20 percent on AI overhead for 265 million monthly users, then rebuilt inference routing, trained in-house models, and added usage limits and pricing controls.
- **[AI is adding to IT workloads, not removing them](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/ai-was-meant-to-simplify-it-service-management-new-research-shows-its-creating-bigger-workloads-for-teams).** SolarWinds found 84 percent say AI met or beat ROI expectations in service management while 52 percent report a heavier overall workload, the time saved on triage going back into integrations, validation, and babysitting the AI.
- **[Claude can now send Gmail replies and manage Drive files](https://x.com/claudeai/status/2089806039088517356).** The expanded connectors move it from retrieval to action, with per-action approval settings, on all paid plans.
- **[Google is giving eligible students a free year of Gemini AI Pro](https://gemini.google/students/).** Higher limits, unlimited course-material uploads, and 5 TB of storage, claimable through December 31, then $19.99 per month unless cancelled.
- **[Tesla's Austin robotaxis are running with no safety monitor](https://thenextweb.com/news/tesla-robotaxi-austin-fully-driverless-bollards).** A crowdsourced tracker logged all 170 rides over two weeks as fully driverless, with roughly 30 driverless Teslas also spotted in Dallas and Houston.
- **[Waymo built its own robocar chip](https://www.theregister.com/edge-and-iot/2026/08/20/waymo-has-designed-a-robocar-chip-to-stay-ahead-of-tesla/5290592).** Its first custom silicon, on TSMC's 5 nm process, is tuned to turn raw sensor data into driving decisions faster.
- **[AI found two distinct centrosome patterns inside breast tumors](https://research.uhs.nhs.uk/news/ai-reveals-hidden-patterns-inside-breast-cancer).** A platform called CenSegNet read 911 samples from 27 patients cell by cell, linking oversized versus extra centrosomes to different outcomes; the system is open-sourced.
- **[Agents were caught cheating a benchmark](https://jumploops.com/blog/sol-loves-to-cheat/).** A developer's agent setup hit 94 percent on Terminal Bench 2.1, then turned out to be gaming the benchmark rather than solving it, an argument for verifying your own evals.
