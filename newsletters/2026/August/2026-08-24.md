## Top Stories

### [Nvidia buys its way up the AI stack](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/nvidia-is-spending-6-billion-to-build-a-powerful-u-s-alternative-to-chinese-ai-c51c38cc)
*(5 newsletters)*

Nvidia spent the week acting less like a chip vendor and more like a holding company for the whole AI supply chain. It agreed to pay $6 billion to license Poolside's model-development technology and hire more than 100 of its engineers onto the Nemotron team, plus a $1 billion equity investment at a $12 billion pre-money valuation, with the stated goal of building a US open-weight alternative to Chinese models. Separately it put $1.5 billion into SoftBank-linked data center developer SB Energy (with up to $105 billion in credit for the OpenAI Ports-Pike facility near Cincinnati) and took a minority stake in Cloverleaf Infrastructure, which assembles the land and power for construction-ready AI factory sites. The through line is that compute, power, and talent are now the constraints, so Nvidia is buying all three rather than waiting for the market to supply them.

### [Slack Code drags coding agents into the group chat](https://slack.com/blog/news/slack-code-channels-for-agents)
*(5 newsletters)*

Slack launched Code, a set of dedicated channels where teams and coding agents (Claude Code, Devin, GitHub Copilot, Vercel) work on a task together instead of each engineer driving an agent alone in a terminal. Agents run with the invoking user's permissions, and the channel preserves diffs, live previews, and activity as a searchable record, then archives itself when the work is done while keeping the context. The pitch that matters for teams like ours is participation: a non-technical stakeholder can watch what an agent is building, click a preview, and leave feedback in the same place the rest of the work already happens.

### [ChatGPT can now read and send your iMessages](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/20/chatgpt-update-adds-apple-messages-integration-on-mac/)
*(4 newsletters)*

OpenAI shipped an Apple Messages plugin for the ChatGPT Mac app that can search, summarize, draft, and send across iMessage, SMS, and RCS. It works inside ChatGPT Work and Codex rather than regular consumer chats, and users have to actively grant consent. OpenAI says processing stays local and messages are not indexed, but the technical detail is thin, and giving a model standing access to an entire personal message history is the kind of permission worth granting narrowly rather than persistently.

### [An anonymous model called Ox Alpha is trading blows with the frontier](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/mystery-ai-model-ox-alpha-draws-developers-with-free-access)
*(3 newsletters)*

A model with no lab attached to it appeared on OpenRouter with a 1M-token context window, multimodal input, and near-unlimited free access, and developers spent the weekend trying to identify who built it. Early benchmarks put it at 80% on a DeepSWE subset, though fuller testing landed closer to 63%, roughly comparable to Fable 5 at far fewer tokens per task. Forensics on its answers and its zodiac-flavored name point at China's Zhipu AI, possibly a GLM release, and all four anonymous OpenRouter drops in the last six months came from Chinese labs. The interesting scenario is not who made it but the size: if something this capable runs locally, the economics of agentic coding change.

### [Humanoid robots beat human world records in Beijing](https://apnews.com/article/china-humanoid-robot-games-us-86cb8e310843151a77057e4cb764b4e2)
*(3 newsletters)*

Chinese humanoid robots broke records set by humans, including Usain Bolt's 100-meter mark, on the opening day of the World Humanoid Robot Games, an event with more than 2,000 robots across 51 events. Unitree claims its new Superman humanoid hits 12.7 m/s against Bolt's 12.4 m/s and clears a two-meter vertical jump, though none of it is independently verified, and the company's Shanghai IPO closed up more than 460% at roughly a $50 billion valuation days earlier. Temper it with the video that also went viral: a sprinting robot lost control in training and snapped at the waist against a padded barrier, and experts still describe these machines as demonstration hardware rather than deployable systems.

### [Moderna and Merck's mRNA cancer vaccine clears Phase 3](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/19/science/moderna-merck-mrna-vaccine-melanoma.html)
*(2 newsletters)*

A personalized mRNA cancer vaccine beat an active standard of care in a late-stage trial for the first time. In a 1,137-patient trial, high-risk melanoma patients given Intismeran Autogene plus Keytruda went significantly longer without recurrence or distant metastasis than those on Keytruda alone. The vaccine is manufactured per patient: doctors sequence the tumor, then encode up to 34 of its mutations into mRNA. Neither company released hazard ratios, confidence intervals, or overall survival data yet, so the size of the benefit is still unknown, but trials in lung, bladder, and kidney cancer are already running.

### [Anthropic ships a learning hub and pushes agent tooling to GA](https://academy.claude.com/)
*(2 newsletters)*

Claude Academy consolidates 355 tutorials, prompting guides, and use-case examples covering Claude, Cowork, Code, Tag, and the API, much of it adapted from Anthropic's internal training material. Alongside it, computer use, the browser tool, and the Skills and Files APIs all moved to general availability, which is the more consequential change: a team can upload a procedure once, pin a version, and reuse file IDs across requests instead of re-sending context. Note that older cached documentation still labels parts of the stack as beta, so availability is worth verifying per account rather than trusting the docs you find first.

### [New York passes the Bay Area in tech talent](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/21/new-york-san-francisco-tech-talent-cbre.html)
*(2 newsletters)*

CBRE's analysis found New York's office market now holds more tech workers than the San Francisco Bay Area, 394,000 against 375,000, the first lead for NYC in the 13 years of the report. The city has added more than 30,000 tech jobs since 2022. The counterweight is capital rather than headcount: California startups have raised a record $366 billion in venture funding so far this year, more than triple the rest of the country combined, with OpenAI and Anthropic alone accounting for over half of it.
