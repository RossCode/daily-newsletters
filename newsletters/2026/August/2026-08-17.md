## Top Stories

### [Google ships Gemini 3.7 Flash at half price](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-3-7-flash/)
*(6 newsletters)*

Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash just three weeks after 3.6 Flash, an unusually short turnaround it attributes to developer feedback and algorithmic gains. The model targets coding, web development, and agentic workflows, with better first-pass code accuracy and stronger multi-step tool calling. Introductory API pricing runs $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens through December 31, then doubles on January 1.

### [Anthropic will watermark Claude's text output](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark)
*(4 newsletters)*

Anthropic confirmed it will embed invisible watermarks in text from Claude models released after August 2, complying with the EU AI Act transparency code. The SynthID-Text method nudges word choices rather than inserting hidden characters, so Anthropic says it adds no cost, no quality hit, and carries nothing traceable to a user or organization. A detection API is coming, though at least one widely shared analysis argues text watermarks of any kind stay trivial to strip.

### [ChatGPT can now watch your Mac and remember it](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/customization/computer-history)
*(4 newsletters)*

OpenAI launched Computer History, an opt-in macOS feature that turns activity in approved apps and websites into memories and a searchable timeline for ChatGPT and Codex. It logs interaction events such as clicks, typing, and app switches rather than screenshots, replacing the earlier Chronicle preview. It is off by default, requires admin approval on Business and Enterprise plans, and is unavailable in the EEA, Switzerland, and the UK.

### [Z.ai's GLM-5.3 posts frontier coding scores, then holds back weights](https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.3)
*(4 newsletters)*

Z.ai released GLM-5.3 built on the same 743B base as GLM-5.2, with all gains coming from expanded post-training: a reported 50% coding improvement and more than double the results on some exploitation tests. It reportedly beats Fable 5 and DeepSeek V4 on Terminal Bench 2.1. The lab is delaying the downloadable weights by two weeks and gating the most sensitive cybersecurity functions, a notable move for a model marketed as open.

### [DeepSeek quadruples API prices and ships an open coding harness](https://www.engadget.com/2236912/deepseek-ai-models-get-four-times-pricier/)
*(3 newsletters)*

DeepSeek announced V4 Pro alongside a 4x API price increase effective August 16, plus new peak and off-peak rates the company frames as resource allocation. The flagship is heavily optimized for agentic workloads. In the same window the lab open-sourced DeepSeek Harness v0.1, a plugin-based alternative to paid coding harnesses, so the cheap-model reputation is now doing something different than it used to.

### [Made by Google turns the Pixel line into Gemini hardware](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/new-pixel-devices-2026/)
*(3 newsletters)*

Google announced the Pixel 11 family from $899 to $1,899 for the Pro Fold, shipping August 20 on Tensor G6, alongside the Pixel Watch 5, a $29 AirTag rival called Pixel Tag, and camera upgrades across the line. The more interesting piece is health: a Health Guardian suite that estimates insulin-resistance trends from heart rate, sleep, activity, and skin temperature with no blood draw or glucose sensor. It reaches back to the $99 screenless Fitbit Air this fall, which moves a once-clinical signal into ordinary wearables.

### [OpenAI previews Ultrafast mode for GPT-5.6 Sol](https://openai.com/index/previewing-ultrafast/)
*(3 newsletters)*

OpenAI previewed Ultrafast, a GPT-5.6 Sol tier generating up to 750 output tokens per second at up to 14x standard speed without falling back to a smaller model. Cerebras is behind it, using its wafer-scale engine and 44GB of on-chip SRAM to remove the memory bandwidth bottleneck. Access is limited to a small group of companies for now, aimed at cases like live outage response and cyberattack triage.

### [Anthropic goes public about what it is not shipping](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/14/anthropic-model-2-ai-risk)
*(2 newsletters)*

Anthropic said it will not release an internal model it claims is more powerful than Mythos, while declining to slow development broadly, and acknowledged it is getting harder to characterize its own models' capabilities. Separately, Dario Amodei made a rare X appearance to push back on claims that his safety warnings have backfired, calling the lock-it-down versus open-model framing a false choice. His argument on public trust was blunt: no lab markets its way out of this, and only real medical breakthroughs shift sentiment.

## Quick Hits

- **The bot economy is worse than analytics show:** PatronView served [1.28 million pages in a week against 5,977 counted pageviews](https://patronview.com/news/99-percent-of-my-website-traffic-is-bots/), roughly 214 invisible loads per visible one. One crawler took 35,000 pages for every visitor it sent back.
- **Compute scarcity is being reframed as permanent:** the [bottleneck is high-bandwidth memory and advanced packaging](https://caseywest.com/compute-scarcity-is-permanent-build-a-ladder/), not budget, and it does not clear with a bigger cloud spend or a patient retry loop.
- **Storage is the quiet AI cost:** [30TB enterprise SSD prices are up roughly 6.5x year over year](https://www.theregister.com/flash/2026/08/11/ssd-prices-still-rocketing-up-faster-than-disk/5286189), changing the economics of large GPU deployments.
- **Understanding, not generation, is the new bottleneck:** two separate widely shared essays argued the same thing, that [humans still need to grasp the code agents write](https://www.geoffreylitt.com/2026/07/02/understanding-is-the-new-bottleneck) and that code is the byproduct of understanding rather than the goal.
- **Figma measured its own AI:** a 100-person randomized trial found [design work 20% faster and 16% easier](https://www.figma.com/blog/measuring-time-savings-from-figma-make/) with Figma Make, with product managers gaining most on simple tasks and designers most on complex ones.
- **Anthropic published research on multi-agent failure modes:** individually benign agent behaviors [compounding into systemic failures](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multiagent-systems) through confabulation, reward hacking, and dynamics that outrun human oversight.
- **AI anxiety has a clinical name now:** Bay Area therapists report ["complicity grief"](https://www.businessinsider.com/silicon-valley-therapists-tech-workers-neuroses-2026-8) among tech workers automating their own roles, in a survey where 55.7% of 8,000-plus respondents reported burnout.
- **Astronomers found something genuinely new:** JWST spotted a [black hole wrapped in a star-like hydrogen cloud](https://news.mit.edu/2026/astronomers-discover-brand-new-type-astrophysical-object-black-hole-star-0812) from a few hundred million years after the Big Bang, emitting roughly 100 billion times more energy than any star should.
- **China's experimental maglev hit [0 to 800 km/h in 5.3 seconds](https://www.independent.co.uk/tech/china-bullet-train-acceleration-record-b3031693.html)** on a 1km test track, the third world record at that facility since 2025.
- **The largest electric plane flew:** Heart Aerospace's X1, with a 106-foot wingspan and over 25,000 pounds, [flew 27 minutes on battery alone](https://www.popsci.com/technology/worlds-largest-electric-plane/) using less than $5 of electricity.
- **Honor's Robot Phone finally shipped:** a [motorized gimbal built into the handset](https://www.honor.com/global/news/honor-robot-phone-launch/) that tracks subjects and shoots autonomously, with sales starting August 18.
- **A warehouse sorting reality check:** one Chinese robot arm sorted [1,816 parcels per hour at 98% accuracy](https://www.wsj.com/business/wonder-takes-a-gamble-on-robots-drones-and-ai-generated-menus-8f26bea6), against roughly 1,250 per hour from three Figure humanoids combined.
- **$9.99 is [the most compelling price in retail](https://www.wsj.com/business/retail/how-9-99-became-the-most-compelling-price-in-retail-ad54da92)**, and inflation is making it so hard to hold that brands are redesigning packaging and accepting lower margins rather than crossing it.
- **The [cheap Vegas buffet is nearly extinct](https://thehustle.co/news/the-last-hurrah-of-the-cheap-vegas-buffet)**, down from about 35 on the Strip to seven. South Point loses $1.5M to $2M a month across its restaurants and keeps going, because the buffet was always a loss leader for the casino floor.
