## Top Stories

### [Google ends its frontier drought with Gemini 4 Argon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/)
*(5 newsletters)*

Google surprised the industry with Gemini 4 Argon, a frontier model built for long-horizon coding, enterprise knowledge work, and defensive cybersecurity, with a 1 million token output limit. It leads the DeepSWE benchmark at 77.9%, and Google says its agents have already freed over 300 TiB of memory across its data centers. Access starts with trusted cyber defenders, then paid API customers and AI Ultra subscribers, at introductory pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output.

### [OpenAI's Dots agents and ChatGPT Space roll out](https://chatgpt.com/features/space/)
*(5 newsletters)*

OpenAI's Dots is an always-on agent platform on GPT-6 Astra that runs its own cloud computer and browser, connects to 4,000+ apps, and works in the background; it is rolling out on Pro, Business Premium, and Enterprise. Alongside it, ChatGPT Space replaces Library with shared living pages where teams co-edit with ChatGPT, Codex, and dots. The Rundown found the Slack integration the easiest agent setup it has tried but advises skipping the desktop app's broad default permissions, and an open-source Mac clone (Open Dot) is already trending.

### [Gemini skills arrive and will replace Gems](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/automate-tasks-with-skills/)
*(3 newsletters)*

Google is rolling out reusable "skills" in Gemini chat, invoked by slash command and able to bundle writing preferences, brand guidelines, and reference files. Skills will gradually replace Gems with automatic migration, and the Workspace rollout begins October 5, so admins should start reviewing existing Gem and Workspace Studio use cases.

### [Personal agents are reshaping commerce](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/no-ads-muse/)
*(3 newsletters)*

Three pieces converged on the same shift: agents like Meta's Muse skip product pages and purchase flows entirely. Amazon blocked Muse while Shopify opened a dedicated payment channel, which positions Shopify to gain from agent-driven transactions while Amazon risks losing default purchases and ad revenue. Meta, meanwhile, can keep ads out of Muse and still monetize every errand as a signal on Facebook and Instagram.

### [Design systems are becoming agent infrastructure](https://southleft.com/insights/design-systems/what-does-ai-readiness-mean-in-design-systems/)
*(3 newsletters)*

TLDR Design, Dev, and Product all ran pieces on design systems as steering layers for AI agents. Testing with the Open Design System Bench found clean separation of concerns and a single source of truth beat extra instruction files, which can hurt when stale. One team rebuilt a 67-page site's design language in three days by keeping content fixed and making the rules numerical.

### [Factory CEO accuses board adviser of spying for Cognition](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/factory-ceo-just-accused-his-vc-board-advisor-of-spying-for-cognition/)
*(2 newsletters)*

Factory's Matan Grinberg said he fired VC Chris Degnan as a board adviser for allegedly leaking confidential information to Cognition. Two hours later Degnan announced he had joined Cognition as CRO and said he had resigned; Cognition's CEO denied receiving any Factory information.

### [OpenAI dismisses three safety researchers over leaks](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/01/openai-cuts-ties-with-three-safety-researchers-wsj-reports/)
*(2 newsletters)*

OpenAI told the WSJ it parted ways with three safety researchers who allegedly passed confidential information to an outside AI safety group. The timing lands two days after a report that executives brushed off staff safety warnings, and amid investigations into rogue agent activity.

### [OpenAI ties distillation campaign to Moonshot AI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/)
*(2 newsletters)*

OpenAI published an investigation attributing a July effort to extract its models' hidden reasoning to people tied to China's Moonshot AI. The campaign used more than 15,000 accounts before it was shut down.

### [Anthropic's leaked prospectus shows the cost of the race](https://www.businessinsider.com/what-smart-people-say-about-anthropics-leaked-ipo-prospectus-2026-9)
*(2 newsletters)*

The leaked IPO prospectus shows $4.6B in 2025 revenue (up 12x), a $42B net loss, and $518B in future compute commitments, though annualized revenue reportedly hit $65B in July. Computerworld argues revenue concentration and a lack of long-term contracts give enterprise CIOs real leverage on price protection, data portability, and model retirement notice.

### [California signs the No Robo Bosses Act](https://qz.com/california-no-robo-bosses-act-ai-worker-firings-100126)
*(2 newsletters)*

Governor Newsom signed SB 947, making California the first state to bar employers from firing or disciplining workers based mainly on automated decisions. A human must now stay in the loop; Newsom had vetoed a similar bill before.

### [Fervo's enhanced geothermal plant goes commercial](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/01/worlds-first-enhanced-geothermal-power-plant-completed-in-just-23-months/)
*(2 newsletters)*

Fervo began selling power from Cape Station on September 30, the first enhanced geothermal developer to reach commercial operation. The first block took 23 months, the site could eventually reach 4 GW, and Google is already a buyer.

### [DeepMind extends SynthID watermarking to proteins](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-synthid-bio/)
*(2 newsletters)*

SynthID Bio tweaks AlphaFold 3 to embed a hidden, function-preserving signature in predicted protein structures that survives into physical form. The goal is to help DNA synthesis suppliers trace unfamiliar AI designs, though Google says tamper resistance is still unsolved.

### [GPT-6 Astra cracks a 217-year-old Napoleonic cipher](https://x.com/CarterWChurch/status/2105167567706816690)
*(2 newsletters)*

An engineer used GPT-6 Astra to decrypt an 1809 military dispatch to Marshal Marmont from a blurry photo in about six hours. It drew 2.5M views and is a tidy demonstration of long-horizon reasoning on messy real-world input.

### [Apple's HomePad reportedly launches October 13](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/30/apples-homepad-smart-home-hub-launching-on-oct-13-bloomberg/)
*(2 newsletters)*

The smart home hub reportedly has a 6-inch display, an iMac G4-inspired design, and a new OS centered on Siri AI, in tabletop and wall-mount versions.

### [Ideogram 4.5 targets precise, repeatable image edits](https://ideogram.ai/models/4.5)
*(2 newsletters)*

The model aims to change only what you ask (product colors, text, photo restoration) while preserving the rest of the image, with high-resolution edits that skip downsizing.
