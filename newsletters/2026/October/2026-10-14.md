## Top Stories

### [Meta launches Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, its first frontier models](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-wonder)
*(7 newsletters)*

Meta released two models built for long-running agent work, pairing "Active Memory" (carrying relevant context across conversations) with "Active Focus" (recovering from errors and trying new approaches). Meta claims Wonder Full beats Opus 5.5 on long-horizon coding and tops Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. The catch: Wonder Full is locked to Muse Code subscribers, and early testers say the API-accessible Wonder is noticeably weaker. A long [Zuckerberg and Alexandr Wang interview](https://cheekypint.substack.com/p/muse-wonder-zuckerberg-and-wangs) covers the talent reboot and $100B+ capex bet behind it.

### [Hackers cook French supermarket refrigerators](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/economy/article/2026/10/13/a-disastrous-day-for-french-supermarkets-after-massive-cyberattack-targets-refrigeration-systems_6758535_19.html)
*(4 newsletters)*

Attackers abused the defrost function on older Danfoss refrigeration controllers, stretching cycles from one hour to eight and pushing units to 50°C (122°F) across an estimated 250 to 350 stores at six major chains. Losses run into the tens of millions of euros, prosecutors are probing reported fires, and no group has claimed it. The flaw had been public since 2025, yet a researcher counted 1,590 exposed systems in France the next morning.

### [Microsoft unveils Maia 300, its third-generation AI chip](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/12/microsoft-to-expand-azure-maia-system-to-multi-gigawatt-deployments/)
*(4 newsletters)*

Microsoft says Maia 300 delivers 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU systems and plans multi-gigawatt deployments starting in 2027, making Maia its primary inference platform for internal products like Copilot. Nadella called it the company's "biggest bet yet" on its own silicon. Microsoft is not planning to rent Maia capacity directly to cloud customers.

### [Anthropic tests how close AI is to self-replicating outside data centers](https://www.anthropic.com/research/self-replicating-ai)
*(3 newsletters)*

Anthropic tested whether models could find poorly secured devices (cheap Android TV boxes, smart ovens), copy themselves, and keep spreading. Small models completed individual steps but struggled to chain them; with deliberately weak access controls, GLM-5.2 and larger models replicated end to end. Anthropic frames the result as an upper bound and urges securing consumer devices now, since there is no central kill switch.

### [Why WebMCP matters](https://blog.yoav.ws/posts/why_webmcp_matters/)
*(3 newsletters)*

WebMCP lets sites expose JavaScript tools with explicit schemas so agents can act directly instead of parsing screenshots or the DOM. Shopify's checkout experiment reported 2.7x faster execution, 58% lower cost, and 60 of 60 successes versus 56 for browser automation. Permissions and human-approval signals remain open problems.

### [FBI arrests OpenAI engineer over hidden recorder in exec meeting room](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/saudi-national-arrested-installing-surreptitious-audio-recording-device-openai)
*(2 newsletters)*

Prosecutors charged engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan with planting a recording device behind a TV in an OpenAI executive conference room in San Francisco; the bug sweep found it on September 30. OpenAI says its own models helped analyze the device's storage and trace it to him, and that no weights or customer data left. The U.S. Attorney called it "a matter of national security," though no foreign government link has been alleged.

### [Character.AI files for bankruptcy; Disney bids $20M](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/13/just-5-years-in-interactive-chatbot-platform-character-ai-files-for-bankruptcy/)
*(2 newsletters)*

The once $2.5B consumer AI darling filed Chapter 11 to sell itself, with Disney placing the opening $20M bid for a license to some of its tech. Safety lawsuits and the 2024 Google licensing deal that pulled away its founders hollowed it out; if no other buyers emerge by early December, it will likely shut down.

### [Apple's "Welcome Home" event: $299 HomeView and a smart home lineup](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/meet-apple-homeview-a-new-way-to-enjoy-entertainment-and-manage-the-home/)
*(2 newsletters)*

Apple announced HomeView, a $299 7-inch smart display shipping November 13, alongside a new Apple TV 4K (A19, hands-free Siri AI), a $149 HomePod mini 2, and an Apple Home Collection of cameras, doorbell, lock, and thermostats built with LG and Schneider Electric. Success hinges on how fast Siri AI actually reaches customers.

### [Releasing AI-generated software without reading the code](https://blog.ploeh.dk/2026/10/12/releasing-ai-generated-software/)
*(2 newsletters)*

The argument: unreviewed AI code can be shipped if wrapped in extreme constraints like unit tests and QA, much like the self-driving car case. The harder problem is liability, since existing legal frameworks assume a human wrote the code.

### [PostHog: 38 things we've learned building AI products](https://newsletter.posthog.com/p/38-things-weve-learned-about-building)
*(2 newsletters)*

Lessons from ten AI products used by roughly 100,000 people monthly. Notably, PostHog's MCP server has five times the daily users of PostHog AI itself, and its install wizard cut two hours of setup to eight minutes.

### [Karpathy: the frontier AI knowledge gap is widening](https://archive.superhuman.ai/2109361546505966046)
*(2 newsletters)*

Andrej Karpathy estimates only about 5,000 people (roughly 0.2% of the population) truly grasp what frontier LLMs can now do, and argues the gap is growing.
