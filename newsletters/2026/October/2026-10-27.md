## Top Stories

### [Anthropic files its S-1 to go public as ANT](https://www.anthropic.com/news/s1-sec)
*(3 newsletters)*

Anthropic filed for a Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT, with a debut possible the week before Thanksgiving and a valuation expected between $1.5T and $2T. The filing shows $16.3B in first-half 2026 revenue (roughly 14x a year earlier), a $974M operating profit in the June quarter, and about $132B of the $141B it has raised still in the bank, though GAAP shows an $80.7B net loss driven by noncash adjustments. Co-founders keep 50.1% of voting power through a Founder LLC and pledged 80% of their stakes to charity; the filing also says Claude wrote over 90% of Anthropic's code in September and warns some Claude-run internal work could become hard for humans to understand and control. Tom Tunguz has a [metrics breakdown](https://tomtunguz.com/anthropics-s1-grew-eleven-fold).

### [Kimi K3.1 Pro pushes open models toward the frontier](https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-k3-1-pro)
*(4 newsletters)*

Moonshot released Kimi K3.1 Pro via API with open weights promised by November 4. It scored 55 on the Artificial Analysis index (4th overall, ahead of Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra) and 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, just behind Claude Opus 5.5, at $2/$10 per million tokens with a 1M-token context. Moonshot says it deliberately removed hacking-related training data to limit offensive cyber skills, and a 2-3x faster Highspeed variant is also available.

### [Block's Dessa: a voice model trained only on speech](https://block.xyz/dessa)
*(3 newsletters)*

Jack Dorsey's Block unveiled Dessa, a full-duplex conversational model trained from scratch on human conversation audio with no text and no external LLM. It handles laughter, sarcasm, pauses, and interruptions, and is small enough to run locally on a MacBook Pro M4 Max or better. Access is limited to safety researchers for now, but the demos are public.

### [OpenAI's models claim to disprove the Exponential Time Hypothesis](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/)
*(2 newsletters)*

OpenAI says unreleased models produced three headline results: a disproof of the Exponential Time Hypothesis (which hundreds of complexity-theory papers rely on), a proof of a 35-year-old wave conjecture, and progress on part of a Millennium Prize problem. The original ETH authors are reviewing the claim, Nobel laureate David Gross is leading the physics review, and OpenAI says it will release raw data and does not seek academic credit. A related essay argues these results show [clever scaffolding keeps getting erased by base-model gains](https://essays.johnloeber.com/p/34-bitter-lessoned-in-automated-theorem).

### [OpenAI hardens its frontier training environments](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/)
*(2 newsletters)*

OpenAI detailed new controls to keep advanced models from escaping training sandboxes, reaching the live internet, or communicating across isolated environments, including red-teaming where agents probe for weaknesses. It lands during what Superhuman calls "Black October," with Anthropic separately showing [formal verification](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification) can rewrite programs with provable security guarantees, while Sam Altman says he no longer believes every bug can be patched.
