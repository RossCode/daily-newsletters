## Top Stories

### [Google makes Gemini 4 Argon broadly available](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/)
*(6 newsletters)*

Google's new flagship model has moved past its early cyber-defender rollout. It is now available through the Gemini API, AI Studio, Antigravity, Google Cloud, and to Pro and Ultra subscribers. It can produce up to 1M output tokens per response and is aimed at long, multi-step work: big codebase refactors, finance, legal, security, and research. Introductory pricing is $2/$10 per million input/output tokens through November 30, then $4/$20. Google says Argon raised internal agentic code submissions by over 35% and cut rollbacks by nearly a third.

### [OpenAI employee arrested for bugging an executive conference room](https://sfstandard.com/2026/10/13/openai-engineer-arrested-alleged-bugging-executive-conference-room/)
*(5 newsletters)*

On September 30, OpenAI found a recording device hidden in toilet paper and plugged into the back of a conference room TV. The room is used for confidential research, business, and government discussions. OpenAI used its own models to analyze the device, which was built to evade detection, then traced it through surveillance footage to product engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan and referred the case to the FBI, which arrested him on October 9. He is charged with intercepting oral communications. OpenAI says it has seen no sign that model weights, source code, or customer data were accessed.

### [Anthropic replaces Git with Scion, an in-house VCS built for agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control)
*(5 newsletters)*

Anthropic went from about 2,500 merges a day in May to more than 30,000 by September, which pushed Git past its limits. A seven-person team built Scion and migrated the whole monorepo in five months. Scion is a centralized system with server-side commits, sparse checkouts, and layered caches, and Anthropic also shipped new code review, code search, and IAM alongside it. It reports roughly 100x faster checkouts, a 200x smaller CPU and memory footprint, and 25% of CI CPU freed up. Anthropic expects other engineering orgs to hit the same wall within a year.

### [Apple unveils HomeView, a $299 smart home display](https://www.apple.com/apple-homeview/)
*(4 newsletters)*

HomeView is a seven-inch screen on a speaker, with countertop and wall-mount versions. It uses facial recognition to tell up to six household members apart and keeps each person's data separate. The new Siri can pull context from each person's paired Apple devices. It ships November 13. Early reactions are mixed, and OpenAI cited the launch in court as possible evidence that Apple used OpenAI's device plans (see below).

### [OpenAI says ex-engineer took 100+ device files back to Apple](https://qz.com/openai-alleges-ex-employee-files-unreleased-device-apple-101426)
*(2 newsletters)*

In a response to Apple's July trade secret suit, OpenAI alleges that a staffer who spent six months there before returning to Apple emailed himself more than 100 confidential files about OpenAI's unreleased device. It also alleges he briefed Apple leadership on OpenAI's product plans. Apple says it found no evidence of this. The headline aside, Judge Edward Davila called a ruling against OpenAI "highly likely," and OpenAI avoided that by agreeing to stay away from the secrets Apple says were taken.

### [Cloudflare: Rewinding the exponential on hardware usage](https://blog.cloudflare.com/rewinding-the-exponential)
*(3 newsletters)*

Cloudflare's data pipeline handles about two billion events per second, and traffic grows faster than hardware capacity. Its approach is to shrink data earlier and duplicate it later: product-specific schemas, zstd compression, sampling at the edge, pre-aggregation for known queries, and replication only after compaction. Together these have put off about two and a half years of hardware expansion. The broader lesson is to optimize bytes, not just compute.

### [How to be more ambitious: a tutorial for humans with models](https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/)
*(3 newsletters)*

Anthropic engineer Felix Rieseberg describes four kinds of ambition with AI: speed (the same work, faster), altitude (aim at the outcome, not the task), depth (work like a field-leading expert, down to the raw data), and range (bring in tools and ideas from other fields). His running example turns "make the quarterly deck" into "find the urgent decisions, test the assumptions, and validate the results with independent tools."

### [The "alien" economics of AI monetization](https://stripe.com/blog/economics-of-ai-monetization)
*(2 newsletters)*

Stripe finds that two-thirds of the Forbes AI 50 use some form of usage-based pricing, because AI costs vary and demand is hard to predict. The leaders change pricing much faster than SaaS companies did, put market share ahead of ARR, and run several go-to-market motions at once. Spending controls, credits, outcome-based charges, and pricing pages that agents can read are becoming part of the product itself.

### [Tech companies are building internal vibe-coding apps](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-new-trend-of-building-internal)
*(2 newsletters)*

Gergely Orosz looked at Ramp's Glass and Stripe's Harbor, which are in-house Lovable-style builders for non-engineers. Nearly every non-engineer at Ramp uses Glass, and building a tool in it is now part of onboarding. The reasons to build rather than buy are safe access to company data, built-in design rules, and low cost: Harbor took one engineer about six weeks. Ramp also reports 6,500+ knowledge edits in Glass and 30% lower AI costs from model routing.

### [Google Play opens to personal vibe-coded apps](https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/10/google-wants-to-host-your-personal-vibe-coded-apps-in-the-play-store/)
*(2 newsletters)*

U.S. users will be able to send apps they build in AI Studio to a private library on the Play Store and run them on their own devices. Sharing comes later, and there will be a reviewed path to the public store. This is the consumer version of the internal-builder trend above.
