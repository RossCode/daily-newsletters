## Top Stories

### [Zuckerberg breaks ranks on the AI slowdown](https://x.com/finkd/status/2099997096896274533)
*(6 newsletters)*

Mark Zuckerberg publicly rejected the coordinated industry pause that Dario Amodei called for over the weekend, arguing every lab already has its own responsibility and incentive to pace itself, and that nobody wants an agent that ignores instructions, so alignment is a selling point rather than a collective-action problem. He pointed to Meta's several-month safety hold on its Muse agent as proof labs self-regulate without being asked, and said Meta is putting the significant majority of its compute toward serving people rather than chasing recursive self-improvement. The scorecard now reads Amodei, Sam Altman, Elon Musk, and Demis Hassabis in favor of slowing down, against Zuckerberg, Jensen Huang, Donald Trump, Beijing, and France's finance minister, who [dismissed](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/calls-slow-down-ai-development-serve-interests-us-ai-leaders-says-french-finance-2026-09-16/) the talk as self-interest from labs at the top of the class.

### [TypeSafe launches Jev, a model that cannot hallucinate](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
*(6 newsletters)*

Diogo Almeida, a co-creator of the methods behind ChatGPT, emerged from two years of stealth with Jev, the first of what TypeSafe calls System One Models. Jev cannot generate text or code at all; it only picks between options defined in advance, returning typed probabilistic decisions with confidence scores in 70 to 500 milliseconds, which the company claims is 40 to 200 times faster and roughly 238 times cheaper than Claude Fable 5.1. The intended use is the background judgment work inside software, such as sorting requests, routing tasks, scoring records, and screening another model's output for jailbreaks. Almeida describes it as more like a database than a coworker.

### [Apple ships iOS 27, and Siri's brain turns out to be swappable](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/major-updates-for-apples-software-platforms-are-now-available/)
*(7 newsletters)*

Apple's annual wave landed across iOS 27, iPadOS, macOS, watchOS, visionOS, and tvOS, with the rebuilt Siri AI running on Google Gemini models, understanding personal context, reading on-screen content, and taking actions inside apps through App Intents. The catch for IT is a hardware divide: Siri AI and the new Apple Intelligence features only run on iPhone 15 Pro, the iPhone 16 line and later, iPhone Air, and iPhone Duo. More interesting than the ship itself, private frameworks in iOS 27 and macOS revealed a [Model Delegation path](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/14/siri-can-be-swapped-out-for-chatgpt-claude/) that lets Claude or GPT-5.6 sit in as Siri's brain while keeping Siri's interface and voice, and an inference-provider hook that can replace Apple's server-side model outright. The hooks are not user-facing yet, but they arrive right after EU DMA pressure to open Siri to rivals.

### [Dreamforce turns Salesforce and Slack into agent surfaces](https://slack.com/blog/news/ai-powered-interface-for-work)
*(5 newsletters)*

Slack expanded well past chat with Slackbot in channels, full-screen AI workspaces, live Salesforce-powered Surfaces that vibe-code interactive reports and dashboards from a plain description, and Slack Code, where developers and agents review code together. Salesforce also trained [Koa](https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/koa), its own in-house reasoning model built on Nvidia's Nemotron 3 Super and trained entirely on synthetic personas, which it says makes three times fewer errors than top models on an internal CRM benchmark while staying hosted inside Salesforce. Separately it opened [Salesforce in Claude](https://claude.com/blog/salesforce-in-claude) in beta on all paid plans, bringing 38 seller skills into Claude under existing Salesforce permissions. The unresolved question for CIOs is economic: as software moves from seat licensing toward agent usage and MCP calls, they gain flexibility and lose cost predictability.

### [Google ships Gemini 3.8 Live and voice models that think while talking](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-8-live-gemini-3-8-live-extended-thinking/)
*(5 newsletters)*

Gemini 3.8 Live and 3.8 Live Extended Thinking collapse the usual voice stack, so you no longer stitch together separate models for transcription, reasoning, and speech. Extended Thinking keeps speaking while it reasons and runs asynchronous tools in the background, which tops Artificial Analysis's speech-to-speech quality ranking but means clients must track interaction status rather than treating turn completion as the end of processing. The same API adds speech translation, text-to-speech, music generation, and coverage across 97 languages. Google also wired Deep Research into Gemini Live, so a report runs in the background and you can then talk through its findings and refine details by voice.

### [Meta puts an agent in the App Store and its best AI behind a paywall](https://webdesignerdepot.com/meta-just-launched-an-ai-that-uses-websites-for-you-that-could-be-a-big-deal-for-web-designers/)
*(4 newsletters)*

Meta's Muse agent navigates websites, fills forms, sends emails, books travel, and makes payments, and it has climbed to number two in the US App Store free rankings behind only ChatGPT with over 83,000 iOS downloads. For anyone building web interfaces, the notable detail is that Muse's browser sub-agent reads an accessibility-tree snapshot rather than the raw DOM, which makes semantic structure suddenly load-bearing. Reuters reported internal testing found reliability problems including an instance where the agent exposed personal iCloud photos. Alongside it, [Meta One](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-meta-one-subscription-service-more-features-ai/) puts AI image editing, video generation, and Instagram's Restyle behind tiers running from $2.99 to $499 a month.

### [Anthropic pushes toward a $2T IPO while merging Claude into one app](https://claude.com/blog/cowork-is-now-claude)
*(4 newsletters)*

Anthropic folded Cowork, Chat, and Artifacts into a single Claude window that routes requests automatically, then added Claude Docs and Slides in beta, producing documents you can comment on and decks you can export to PowerPoint. On the finance side it is pressing ahead with a Nasdaq listing at a reported $2 trillion valuation, with Nvidia reportedly weighing roughly $10 billion into the offering. Two claims have drawn scrutiny: a report that Anthropic has been profitable two quarters running excludes training costs and shared revenue, and the company told investors it sees a $30 trillion revenue opportunity, roughly a quarter of global GDP, against $65 billion in annualized revenue this year. It also [partnered with Novo Nordisk](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/novo-will-work-with-anthropic-ai-to-speed-drug-development) to put Claude Science into drug research.

### [Google Home opens an MCP server to any agent](https://developers.home.google.com/mcp/home)
*(3 newsletters)*

Google opened early access to a Model Context Protocol server for Google Home, so any MCP-capable agent, Claude and ChatGPT included, can manage devices, read event history, summarize camera footage across rooms, and build smart home dashboards in plain language. It is US-only, English-only, and limited to Google Home Premium Advanced users, and setup still requires spinning up a Google Cloud project. Safety rails prohibit sensitive actions like unlocking doors, but Google warns that behavior can still be unexpected or undesired depending on which agent is driving.

*(2 newsletters)*

OpenRouter users spent more on OpenAI models than on Anthropic models last week, the first time that has happened in more than two and a half years, driven by surges in GPT-5.6 and GPT-6 Astra usage. OpenAI is pressing the advantage by retiring GPT-5.5 from Codex, ChatGPT, and Work on October 14, steering teams toward Sol or Astra. Separately, OpenAI has held early talks with investors about a pre-IPO round valuing it at more than $1.2 trillion, delayed in part by AI safety concerns, with more than a billion active users and 200 million businesses on its models.
