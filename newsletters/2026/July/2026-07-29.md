## Top Stories

### [Moonshot releases full open weights for Kimi K3](https://www.kimi.com/blog/kimi-k3)
*(4 newsletters)*

Moonshot AI published the weights and technical report for Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with native visual understanding and a 1-million-token context window, making it the largest open-weight model ever released. The lab claims a new architecture delivering 2.5x more intelligence per unit of compute, and is also opening parts of the surrounding stack including attention kernels and a MoE communication library. At that size almost no one will run it locally, so the practical path is cheap hosted inference and distillation into smaller models, which puts real pricing pressure on the US frontier labs.

### [Microsoft ships a dedicated cybersecurity model and agentic security platform](https://microsoft.ai/news/introducing-mai-cyber-1-flash-inside-mdash/)
*(4 newsletters)*

Microsoft introduced MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, a model built specifically to find hard vulnerabilities in large codebases, running inside a multi-agent harness called MDASH. It reportedly scores 96% on CyberGym at roughly half the cost of leading general-purpose models, handling routine scans autonomously and escalating only the toughest tasks. The related Project Perception platform coordinates red, blue, and green team agents across identities, endpoints, clouds, and AI systems, and enters public preview on August 3.

### [Anthropic rejects blanket bans on open-weight models](https://www.anthropic.com/news/position-open-weights-models)
*(3 newsletters)*

Anthropic published its position after more than 130 companies signed a letter opposing restrictions on open-weight models, with Dario Amodei saying the company has never advocated such bans and that less capable open releases are a public good. Instead it wants tighter chip export controls, action against industrial-scale distillation, and mandatory safety testing for any sufficiently capable model whether open or closed. The framing shifts the policy fight from openness itself toward capability thresholds and hardware access.

### [NVIDIA leads a new Open Secure AI Alliance](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/open-secure-ai-alliance/)
*(3 newsletters)*

NVIDIA, Microsoft, and dozens of other technology and security companies formed the Open Secure AI Alliance to build shared open-source tooling for defending AI systems. Members plan to ship models, agent harnesses, identity controls, scanners, and secure coding workflows that defenders can inspect and run on their own infrastructure rather than trusting closed vendor stacks. The alliance is explicitly lobbying policymakers to treat open models and tools as a cybersecurity asset, which lands directly in the middle of the open-weights debate above.

### [Anthropic model finds flaws in tough-to-crack encryption](https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses)
*(2 newsletters)*

Claude Mythos Preview found weaknesses in a reduced-round version of AES, with Anthropic claiming the attack was 200 to 1,000 times faster than prior human research. The model worked on the problem for about a week and devised the attack almost entirely autonomously, though it initially believed the task was impossible and two human researchers spent nearly a month verifying the result. In separate work the same model spent 60 hours halving the effective strength of a quantum-resistant algorithm, and neither result threatens deployed systems today.

### [Apple retakes the most-valuable-company crown and briefly tops $5T](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/27/apple-most-valuable-company-nvidia.html)
*(2 newsletters)*

Apple closed at a record high Monday, passing Nvidia for the first time in 13 months at roughly $4.93T against Nvidia's $4.78T, then touched a $5 trillion intraday market cap Tuesday to become only the second company ever to do so. Apple is up about 24% in 2026 versus 5 to 6% for Nvidia, whose latest slide followed reports it may guarantee around $250 billion in financing for OpenAI data centers. The reversal suggests investors are rewarding a capital-light approach of putting AI into devices and services over matching the data center spending race.

### [Zuckerberg argues superintelligence should be for everyone](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/the-ai-future-is-for-everyone-a0c24e20)
*(2 newsletters)*

In a Wall Street Journal op-ed, Mark Zuckerberg treats superintelligence within the next few years as a given and argues the real risk is extreme concentration of power rather than wide access. He predicts more jobs rather than fewer if capability spreads, says invention rather than automation will be the technology's main contribution, and questions why anyone convinced AI erases jobs would keep building it. Notably he never uses the phrase open source, and Meta's real lever may be distribution through apps billions already open daily.

### [OpenAI data shows workers using AI far outside their job descriptions](https://openai.com/index/how-ai-is-expanding-what-people-do-at-work/)
*(2 newsletters)*

An OpenAI analysis of more than 800,000 work-related ChatGPT messages found that 43.5% of occupation-specific usage involved tasks normally belonging to another profession. Customer experience workers showed the highest crossover at 77%, with marketing tasks, engineering troubleshooting, and financial calculations traveling widely across roles, and smaller businesses crossing boundaries more than large ones. The practical read is that AI is not just speeding existing work but quietly redrawing where one job ends and another begins.

### [Nvidia invests in Safe Superintelligence](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/27/ilya-sutskevers-safe-superintelligence-partners-with-nvidia-to-scale-its-ai-research/)
*(2 newsletters)*

Nvidia is putting up to $5 billion into Ilya Sutskever's secretive Safe Superintelligence after the lab confirmed it has research worth scaling, alongside a long-term partnership giving it access to the Vera Rubin GPU platform. It is the clearest signal yet from a lab that has published almost nothing since founding.

### [Recursive Superintelligence signs a $410M compute deal with AWS](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/recursive-superintelligence-signs-400-compute-deal-with-amazon/)
*(2 newsletters)*

The lab, which left stealth in May with $650 million in funding, will use the multiyear AWS deal to scale its open-ended self-improving systems. It is another data point in how quickly compute commitments are being locked up by labs with barely any public track record.
