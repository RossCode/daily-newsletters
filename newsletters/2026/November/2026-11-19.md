## Top Stories

### [Google ships Gemini 4 Neon, its fourth Gemini 4 model in under two months](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/)
*(6 newsletters)*

Neon sits between Carbon and Hydrogen but scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, edging Carbon and trailing only Claude Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2 Astra. Google says it beats Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2 Sol on 16 of 19 benchmarks, with a 1M-token context window and pricing of $3/$15 per million tokens, about a quarter below Opus 5.5; it is live in the API, Antigravity, and paid Gemini plans. The Rundown frames it as Google suddenly "very back," with one Google researcher hinting at recursive self-improvement behind the pace, while TLDR AI and TLDR Dev ran a counterpoint on [Gemini 4 Carbon taking roughly seven times as many steps as Astra](https://kernel.dev/blog/why-is-gemini-4-carbon-taking-so-long/) on bug fixes, about half of them unnecessary.

### [Anthropic raises $81.3B in the largest IPO on record, and critics show up on day one](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-ipo-ai-offering-price.html)
*(4 newsletters)*

Anthropic sold about 980 million shares at $83, giving it a fully diluted market cap near $1.5 trillion; it starts trading on Nasdaq today as ANT. In parallel, nearly 90 signatories including Thomas Wolf, Igor Babuschkin, and Dan Hendrycks published [The MisAnthropic Statement](https://misanthropicstatement.com/), arguing the lab's pursuit of recursive self-improvement contradicts its safety branding. Superhuman notes the S-1 devoted more than 90 pages to risk factors, and TLDR AI flagged Epoch data showing Anthropic's [compute spend growing about 4x per year](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/anthropic-openai-compute-spend-growth), with nearly $10B spent in Q3 alone.

### [Microsoft Ignite: seven cheap models, Copilot Workspaces, and a pile of agent governance](https://microsoft.ai/news/microsoft-ignite-2026/)
*(5 newsletters)*

Microsoft AI released seven models built for price-performance, including MAI Code 2 (now in GitHub Copilot, about 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol's coding quality at 40% of the price) and MAI Image 2.7. On the enterprise side it showed multiplayer Copilot Workspaces, Copilotforce integrations with Salesforce, [tighter Windows controls for AI agents](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsexperience/2026/11/17/microsoft-ignite-2026-building-windows-for-the-next-era-of-work/) (process containers, hardware-isolated micro VMs, Entra Agent ID), Teams impersonation protection, and an industry AI Transparency Initiative. The through-line across TLDR IT's two issues: Microsoft is positioning Windows as the governed runtime for enterprise agents.

### [OpenAI releases GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol to mixed reviews](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/)
*(4 newsletters)*

Astra is the new frontier model and Sol the efficient everyday one; both are in ChatGPT Work, Codex, and the API, promise fewer output tokens, and Astra gets a 20% price cut to $8/$40 per million tokens. It ranks #2 on Artificial Analysis, but early reviews are split: some devs see cleaner code and better reviews, while one CTO says Astra stalls on long tasks and asks baffling questions. The Code followed up with a one-shot prompt for auditing a Codex config that is still tuned for GPT-6.

### [OpenAI pushes ChatGPT deeper into business workflows](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-for-sales/)
*(5 newsletters)*

ChatGPT for Sales adds a Sales plugin plus 30+ new and upgraded integrations that pull context from Salesforce, HubSpot, and similar tools for call prep, proposals, and follow-ups. OpenAI also launched [ChatGPT for Accounting](https://openai.com/business/solutions/accounting/) for invoicing and month-end reporting, and Live Dictation now transcribes in real time across all plans with the old 10-minute limit removed. Taken together, it is a clear move from general chat toward packaged, role-specific workflows.

### [A week of cyber incidents: Optimizely hijacked, Apple and NSA systems go dark](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/11/17/optimizely-hack-turns-worlds-biggest-websites-into-malware-dispensers/5310448)
*(3 newsletters)*

Attackers abused Optimizely's experimentation software to inject malicious JavaScript into sites at 178 organizations including Zoom, CNN, the BBC, Salesforce, and Equifax, targeting crypto wallets and payment details. Separately, Apple's sign-ins, VPNs, App Store, and iCloud went down for hours (Apple's own status page stayed green), and the NSA's internal network reportedly failed; officials insist the outages were internal updates, not attacks. Google's Threat Intelligence Group says [agentic attack activity has crossed a significant threshold](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/agentic-threat-activity-crosses-a-significant-threshold), with autonomous exploits outpacing patch cycles.

### [Gemini Tasks can now make calls, book appointments, and pay bills](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/)
*(3 newsletters)*

Google expanded Tasks, a Gemini mode that works in the background across Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive, and Calendar instead of chatting turn by turn. It can call businesses, manage calendars, and complete purchases via Google Pay within a spending limit, powered by Gemini 4 Hydrogen, and is rolling out across most plans. Josh Woodward says he uses it to sort medical bills and chase down a spiking electric bill.

### [Genie 4 lets you build and edit interactive worlds by talking](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/genie-4-now-available-project-genie/)
*(3 newsletters)*

DeepMind opened Genie 4 in Project Genie to eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers, with Pro users on a waitlist. Users can change weather, add characters, and hear generated audio and dialogue while exploring, then remix worlds others have made. It is still experimental, but AI Entrepreneurs points to early use for filmmakers, game designers, and educators prototyping ideas.

### [Nvidia moves forward without Jensen Huang](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/nvidia-interim-ceo-kress-addresses-staff-after-jensen-huang-exit-.html)
*(3 newsletters)*

Huang's first public statement since resigning drew 30M+ views and closed with an offer the board has reportedly already declined, amid a new SEC probe. At her first all-hands, interim CEO Colette Kress told staff "let's move forward" and pulled raises and annual reviews forward from March to December. There is visible internal division over reinstating Huang, but the new board chair says the board does not intend to.

### [How the Claude Code team thinks about verification](https://claude.dev/blog/how-the-claude-code-team-thinks-about-verification/)
*(3 newsletters)*

With roughly 90% of Claude Code's code written and reviewed by Claude, the team treats verification as running the changed system and collecting evidence through end-to-end, property, and differential tests. Review rules live outside the change being judged, cheap checks run before shared CI, and recurring misses become rules tested against past incidents. It pairs well with SpaceXAI engineer Lauren Tan's talk (The Code) on [fixing the environment rather than the prompt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O_zcc6Xl12g) to ship 3,000 agent PRs a month.

### [Decade-old NGINX HTTP/2 bug allows unauthenticated remote code execution](https://www.wiz.io/blog/nginx-http2-rce-vulnerabilities)
*(2 newsletters)*

Wiz found two memory-safety flaws in NGINX's HTTP/2 code dating to 2015 using its AI code scanner; Xint found them independently and chained one with a Linux kernel bug for root. No exploitation has been reported yet, but this is a patch-now item; remediation steps are at [nginx2root.com](https://nginx2root.com/#fix).

### [Prime Intellect launches Model Factory for in-house training](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/introducing-model-factory)
*(2 newsletters)*

Model Factory puts RL, fine-tuning, pre-training, and inference in one control plane so companies can train an open-weights model on their own tasks, deploy it with one click, and keep improving it from production results. Billing is per GPU-hour, and it is already used for finance automation, computer use, and chip design.

### [Vercel ships eve 1.0, an open-source framework for durable agents](https://vercel.com/blog/eve-is-now-generally-available)
*(2 newsletters)*

eve manages the model-and-tool loop plus memory, approvals, and monitoring. The 1.0 release adds multiplayer sessions, broader framework integrations, OpenTelemetry traces, and workflow checkpointing so interrupted work can resume.
