## Top Stories

### [Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director)
*(5 newsletters)*

Nvidia's board voted unanimously to bring Jensen Huang back as president, CEO and director just 11 days after he resigned, and six days after the new chair said he would not return. The board said senior US government officials weighed in, and President Trump told [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/trump-nvidia-jensen-huang.html) he personally lobbied for it; shares jumped more than 30% at the open, adding over $1T in market value. Huang still faces a House hearing on December 1, Democrats are calling the move indefensible, and the [WSJ notes](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/jensen-huang-is-back-at-nvidia-major-investigations-still-surround-him-c904001c) the investigations surrounding him have not gone away.

### [Meta's AI chief: "Superintelligence is arriving" after the Claude-assisted Riemann proof](https://x.com/alexandr_wang/status/2124636606959345776)
*(6 newsletters)*

Levent Alpöge's 17-million-line Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, built with Claude, has been [certified by the Lean and Mathlib communities](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/), and Alpöge says Claude "far outpaced" him on the home stretch. Meta Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang responded with an essay arguing intelligence no longer limits AI, that models will soon run AI research themselves, and that the industry should reject the "default path" toward a handover to AI. The Rundown notes how far this departs from Zuckerberg's usual dismissal of slowdown talk.

### [Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Mini, a laptop-sized open model](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
*(5 newsletters)*

Mira Murati's lab shipped a 26.4B-parameter open-weight model that loops each token through its middle layers three times, letting it reason more while running in about 20GB on a 32GB Mac. It leads all open models under 100B on DeepSWE (48.2%), and Artificial Analysis says it matches Qwen3.8-27B while finishing tasks four times faster at one-sixth the cost ($0.15/$0.50 per 1M tokens). Factual recall is weak, so pair it with search; it is also one of the few US answers to China's open-weight lead.

### [OpenAI publishes pretraining scores and asks rivals for shared limits](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/)
*(3 newsletters)*

Chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published held-out log perplexity for every major OpenAI model since GPT-3.5 and proposed using perplexity alongside other measures to set industry-wide safety thresholds that labs agree not to cross. Sam Altman called it the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while, though OpenAI concedes different evaluation datasets make cross-lab comparisons hard.

### [Companies are building "knowledge factories" for their agents](https://www.notion.com/a-new-shape-for-work)
*(3 newsletters)*

LangChain's Harrison Chase and Notion co-founder Akshay Kothari both argue that the bottleneck for workplace agents is shared, curated company knowledge rather than more chatbot sessions. Chase's recipe: capture skills, domain knowledge and data access; give agents a way to flag gaps; run a separate "curator agent" to update the knowledge base; and keep working agents from editing it directly. Kothari's viral guide (6.5M views) makes the same case under the name "context system."

### [Anthropic launches Claude Ship for automated PR review](https://claude.com/resources/articles/introducing-claude-ship)
*(3 newsletters)*

Claude can now review, fix and approve pull requests when a team's policy allows it, with agents handling routine fixes in GitHub. Coverage frames it as Claude getting a permanent green light on code review.

### [OpenAI's OpenWorks pays locals to bring AI to small businesses](https://openai.com/openworks/)
*(2 newsletters)*

A pilot in 20 US cities, 15 of them in the Midwest or South, pays tech-savvy partners stipends of up to $8,000 a month to work hands-on with restaurants, shops, factories and service firms and teach a monthly community class. Businesses get three free months of ChatGPT Business; software, VC-backed and PE-owned companies are excluded. It doubles as a paid on-ramp into AI consulting and a pipeline for OpenAI's Business tier.

### [Amazon buys Decart's AI tech for $7.5B ahead of selling Trainium](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/amazon-snaps-up-decarts-ai-optimization-tech-for-7-5b-as-it-prepares-to-sell-trainium-chips/)
*(2 newsletters)*

Amazon is acquiring the Israeli startup's optimization software, which helps models run efficiently across Nvidia, Amazon and Google hardware, along with its founders and researchers. The timing lines up with Amazon preparing to sell Trainium chips directly as an Nvidia rival.

### [Agentic shopping takes off before the holidays](https://www.a16z.news/p/welcome-to-the-b2a-economy-how-agents)
*(3 newsletters)*

Daily spend through Stripe's Link Agent Wallet is up nearly 80x since September, led by airfare and retail, and more than half of first-time agentic buyers come back. a16z argues sellers must make strengths, costs and policies easy for agents to evaluate. Starting November 24, Wells Fargo holds customers responsible for their agents' actions, so permission and revocation controls now matter for builders.

### [Watching real users is how you make products better](https://jan.miksovsky.com/posts/2026/11-23-observing-users.html)
*(2 newsletters)*

Jan Miksovsky's essay argues observing someone attempt realistic tasks surfaces problems no amount of careful design thinking will, and that fear of that feedback is a reliable path to bad products.
