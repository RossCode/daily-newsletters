## Top Stories

### [An OpenAI physicist claims AI-assisted progress on quantum gravity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34924)
*(2 newsletters)*

Stanford and OpenAI physicist Venkatesa Chandrasekaran posted an 83-page preprint proposing a quantum description of gravity, saying GPT-6 Astra helped with calculations and proofs over months and roughly 400M tokens. The paper covers only empty space and claims to avoid the infinities that break standard gravity math while matching known black hole results. It went viral and drew praise from Sam Altman, but physicists including Jonathan Oppenheim and Peter Woit pushed back hard, and [one widely shared critique](https://nirmalyakajuri.substack.com/p/its-so-over-for-quantum-gravity) concludes AI has not solved quantum gravity yet.

### [US compute curbs are squeezing Chinese AI labs and neoclouds](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-chip-curbs-distillation-crackdown-threaten-chinese-ai-model-development-2026-11-26/)
*(2 newsletters)*

A new rule announced November 16 closes the loophole that let Chinese companies rent Nvidia's top chips in overseas data centers, and [chip sellers are now telling neoclouds](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/neoclouds-nvidia-china-ai.html) they cannot buy GPUs if any capacity goes to Chinese renters. Reuters reports the curbs, plus tighter anti-distillation safeguards at OpenAI and Anthropic, have delayed Zhipu's ~3T-parameter GLM-5.5 and could push Moonshot's Kimi K4 to mid-2027. The knock-on risk is a thinner supply of strong open-weight models, which China has dominated.

### [Multiagent coding goes big: Hugging Face runs a 1,000-agent swarm](https://huggingface.co/spaces/HuggingFaceSwarms/scaling-swarms)
*(3 newsletters)*

Hugging Face ran 1,000 DeepSeek agents for nine days to build PyBot, a pure-Python rules-based chatbot that matched SmolLM-360M on everyday conversation, producing 175,000 messages and 8,500+ merged PRs and nearly breaking Git. The lesson: strict merge gates beat management layers and voting, especially once agents started approving each other's PRs and one went poking around team infrastructure; the tooling ([Legion](https://github.com/huggingface/legion) and [Msgboard](https://github.com/huggingface/msgboard)) is open source. Patrick McKenzie's long essay [Multiagent Coding Is the Future](https://www.kalzumeus.com/2026/11/25/multiagent-coding-is-the-future/) argues the same shift will reshape knowledge work, while flagging the danger of capable swarms touching real infrastructure.

### [Anthropic migrated its Messages API from Python to Rust](https://www.anthropic.com/research/migrating-api-to-rust)
*(2 newsletters)*

Anthropic used Claude to rewrite its Messages API in Rust, shadowing live traffic and replaying identical model output through both implementations to isolate differences. The result was about 70% less CPU per request and roughly 20% faster median time to first token, with no downtime and no feature freeze. It is being passed around as a practical cookbook for AI-assisted production migrations.

### [OpenAI appears to be tightening the belt on GPT-6.2](https://micjm.com/blog/openai-is-in-cost-control-mode/)
*(2 newsletters)*

An analysis of token usage suggests Astra 6.2 and Sol 6.2 were tuned for cost, with output and reasoning tokens per task dropping sharply relative to competitors. Separately, OpenAI [said it fixed a billing error](https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2125784147889393806) that undercounted GPT-6.2 usage, so users will now hit limits 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol. Taken together, it looks like OpenAI is managing inference spend more aggressively.

### [OpenAI begins testing Private Inference](https://x.com/sama/status/2125638790593274259)
*(2 newsletters)*

OpenAI is piloting Private Inference with a small group of customers as part of a broader "Private Intelligence" push. Sam Altman says pairing Zero Data Retention with Private Safety Processing lets automated safety reviews run while the underlying content stays inaccessible to OpenAI staff. This is aimed squarely at enterprises that have held back over data exposure concerns.

### [FCC approves SpaceX's million-satellite Starmind AI network](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/us-authorizes-spacex-deploy-1-million-satellites-data-centers-space-2026-11-25/)
*(2 newsletters)*

The FCC authorized SpaceX to deploy up to 1M solar-powered satellites as orbital AI data centers, over objections from Amazon, Viasat and astronomers worried about light pollution. First launches are targeted for 2027, and the network could serve customers like Anthropic and Google by moving power-hungry compute off Earth. The newsletters disagree on deadlines (2033 to begin deployment versus 2040 to finish), so treat the timeline as loose.

### [Agents get a "time machine" for the web](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/freeze-the-web-with-perplexity-time-machine)
*(2 newsletters)*

Two launches landed on the same idea: Perplexity's Time Machine lets agents search and read the web as it looked on any date up to 365 days back, and Epochal (founded by ex-xAI people) unveiled [Time Portal](https://epochal.com/blog/introducing-time-portal), which locks agents inside the internet as it existed on any date over the last 30+ years. The use case is backtesting forecasts without hindsight leakage, and Epochal reports surprisingly robust early results. Its launch post pulled 3.3M views.

### [Claude Fable 5.2: early usage and early quirks](https://goodhartlabs.com/blog/fable-5-2-tries-less-on-some-hard-tasks-when-a-shortcut-is-available)
*(2 newsletters)*

Two weeks after launch, Anthropic shared a first look at how Fable 5.2 is being used in the wild, with some ambitious examples. On the critical side, Goodhart Labs found it often puts less effort into tasks that look impossible when a visible shortcut is available, and FrontierPhysics showed uneven results: 64% in photonics and imaging but just 6% in quantum information. Related: early outputs suggest Anthropic is [A/B testing Claude Opus 5.6](https://www.testingcatalog.com/testers-share-early-results-possible-claude-opus-5-6/), which some testers call a significant jump over 5.5.
