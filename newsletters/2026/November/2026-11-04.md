## Top Stories

### [OpenAI is merging ChatGPT's Chat and Work tabs](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/openai-is-finally-merging-chatgpts-chat-and-work-tabs/)
*(4 newsletters)*

OpenAI is folding ChatGPT Work's file, plugin and web-tool capabilities into regular Chat, starting with select users, while Codex stays a separate section of the desktop app. ChatGPT and Codex will now draw from one shared usage allowance, which may mean more frequent waits for resets for heavy users. In a [Big Technology interview](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/openai-chatgpt-search-is-about-20), consumer lead Adam Fry said ChatGPT added 200 million users in two months, its search volume is about 20% of Google's, and users make 1.5 billion images a week.

### [MIT: frontier models can edit other models' weights directly](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/)
*(3 newsletters)*

MIT researchers let GPT-6 Astra rewrite a small Qwen model's weights like code, and a single targeted edit noticeably improved its math skills. The not-yet-peer-reviewed paper drew millions of views. A [counterpoint from Sean Goedecke](https://www.seangoedecke.com/llms-are-not-superhuman-at-reading-and-writing-neural-network-weights/) argues the result reflects skilled-researcher-level problem solving, not a fundamentally new AI capability.

### [Apple opens Siri AI to all developers](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=gdk2umrf)
*(2 newsletters)*

A developer preview lets any app expose actions to Siri AI through App Intents and teach it multi-step "skills," and Siri can now surface any entity in the Spotlight semantic index. It is a real crack in Apple's walled garden, but App Store submissions don't open until spring, so customers are months away. The same betas bring China its first Apple Intelligence features (minus Siri AI).

### [CrowdStrike spends ~$500M on AI agent security and ships a "kill switch"](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/crowdstrike-introduces-ai-kill-switch/)
*(2 newsletters)*

CrowdStrike is [buying Fiddler AI and Dreadnode](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/crowdstrike-to-shell-out-nearly-500m-for-2-ai-security-startups-as-the-market-for-securing-agents-takes-off/) for roughly $400M and $95M, and added an AI Kill Switch to Falcon Guardian that cuts an agent's link to its model on a human admin's command. It also added Claude Cowork protection in beta. Separately, it [partnered with Dell](https://ir.crowdstrike.com/news-releases/news-release-details/crowdstrike-and-dell-technologies-announce-integrated-ai) to bundle Falcon with the Dell AI Factory.

### [OpenAI rebuilt its Git backend because agents broke it](https://openai.com/index/scaling-git-at-openai/)
*(2 newsletters)*

Codex agents were pushing so much code that OpenAI's Git reliability sometimes dipped below 90%. Its new internal server, Pando, built in 10 weeks largely by Codex itself, cut median push latency about 80% and serves nearly a petabyte of Git data a day. Pando stays internal, but OpenAI is publishing the fixes.

### [DeepSeek launches Creator Mode for building plugins by prompt](https://www.deepseek.com/en/harness/)
*(2 newsletters)*

DeepSeek's open-source Harness takes an "everything is a plugin" approach, and Creator Mode lets you describe a tool, skill or interface tweak and have the agent build it into the platform. Demos range from notes apps to design consoles. It is still in preview, so expect API changes.

### [Google will cut off Search for schools that turn off AI](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/03/us/google-search-schools-restrict-ai.html)
*(2 newsletters)*

K-12 student accounts will start with Search, Gemini and Notebook all off early next year, but districts that keep AI off also lose Google Search, since Google says the two are "deeply integrated." The move came a day after a Senate deadline on Google's classroom AI push, and Lina Khan called it using a search monopoly to coerce schools.

### [Grok Bot's costs are "unsustainably high," says Musk](https://www.superhuman.ai/p/deepseek-s-customization-skills-ramp-up-pressure-on-us-labs)
*(2 newsletters)*

Two weeks after calling it the fastest-growing product he'd seen, Musk confirmed Grok Bot usage limits were cut because serving costs outran revenue. It's a reminder that always-on personal agents are expensive and labs are likely subsidizing them.

### [BrainCo wants a wearable "electronic GLP-1"](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/neuralink-china-brainco-obesity-with-electronic-glp-wearable-weight-loss)
*(2 newsletters)*

The Chinese neurotech firm, often called China's Neuralink, is building a brain-stimulation device to suppress appetite, targeting a China launch in two to three years before a possible US expansion. It has not shown large-trial weight-loss results, and stimulation may not replicate GLP-1 drugs' broader effects.

### [New benchmarks: agents still struggle on hard, real work](https://gdpx.semianalysis.com/)
*(2 newsletters each)*

SemiAnalysis's GDPx tested agents on 206 difficult professional tasks: Claude Opus 5.5 led at just 12%, ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon (10.9%) and GPT-6 Astra (9.6%). Meanwhile OpenAI's [LifeVal](https://openai.com/index/lifeval/) measures everyday tasks like budgeting, finding childcare and accessing government services, built from ChatGPT user feedback.

### [Our organizations are being DDoSed from the inside](https://karrisaarinen.com/posts/our-organizations-are-being-ddosed-from-the-inside/)
*(2 newsletters)*

Linear's Karri Saarinen argues AI makes changes cheap to generate, but reviewing, understanding and deciding on them still draws on the same finite human attention. Output is not proof of value, and some changes are net negative. His fix: clearer intent on priorities and a deliberate gate between experiments and the shared product.

### [A rough week for software security](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-time-to-rethink-software)
*(2 newsletters)*

Over 1,000 internet-facing GitLab servers remain exposed to an actively exploited max-severity RCE (CVE-2026-97229), now in CISA's KEV catalog. The Pragmatic Engineer tallies the broader pile-up (a CA issuing guessable keys, PHP and Node.js flaws, Railway leaking secrets, a month-long TiDB breach) and argues AI makes exploits easier enough that security will cost more. [WorkOS also disclosed](https://workos.com/security/bulletins/october-30th-2026-data-security-incident) a breach of an internal sales app by an AI-assisted attack framework.
